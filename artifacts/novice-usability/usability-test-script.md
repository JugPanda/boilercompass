# BoilerCompass novice usability-session script

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a Purdue student seeing BoilerCompass for the first time can understand the product, judge source trust appropriately, find the right destination in student language, and predict what happens before opening a link.

This script is for moderated sessions with people who did not build BoilerCompass. Aim for at least five participants, including a mix of newer and experienced Purdue students when available.

## Consent and privacy note

Read before beginning:

> We are testing BoilerCompass, not you. You may stop at any time or skip any task. Please do not sign in, enter a Purdue password, share academic records, disclose health details, or provide other sensitive information. We will record task outcomes, navigation choices, time, errors, hesitations, and comments—not account credentials or private student information.

Record verbal consent: **Yes / No**

If consent is No, end the session without collecting task data.

## Moderator guidance

- Begin from a fresh browser profile or clear BoilerCompass local storage.
- Ask the participant to think aloud.
- Do not teach BoilerCompass terminology or name the expected destination.
- Do not reveal the answer embedded in the observation notes.
- If the participant is stuck for 60 seconds, ask: “What would you try next?”
- Stop before login and external form submission.
- For crisis guidance, use a hypothetical scenario. Do not ask about the participant’s health or personal experiences.

## Participant introduction

> Imagine you found this site while trying to complete normal Purdue student tasks. Use the site as you naturally would. Tell me what you expect before selecting a link and what makes you trust or distrust a result.

## First-impression questions

Allow 10 seconds on the homepage, then ask:

1. What does this site help you do?
2. Is it an official Purdue website? What tells you that?
3. What would you do first if you needed help with a student task?

## Tasks

Do not read the expected result or success criteria to participants.

### Task 1 — Coursework

> You need to view materials and assignments for one of your classes. Find where you would go.

Success: identifies Brightspace, understands it is official and requires Purdue login, and predicts that the external action opens Brightspace.

### Task 2 — Tutoring

> You want academic tutoring or study help, but you do not know the office name. Find a useful starting point.

Success: finds the Tutoring & help-center directory or another accurate official academic-support destination using task language.

### Task 3 — BoilerClasses trust

> You found BoilerClasses and want to use it for planning. Determine whether Purdue officially operates it and find an official source you could use to verify decisions.

Success: identifies Purdue-affiliated status, understands it is not the official registration/degree-audit source, and finds Purdue Catalog or myPurdue.

### Task 4 — Change a major

> Find official information about changing your major and explain what CODO means.

Success: finds CODO requirements, expands the acronym, and notices that current criteria/advisor confirmation matter.

### Task 5 — Billing

> Find where you would pay or understand a Purdue bill.

Success: finds the Office of the Bursar or myPurdue billing path and recognizes Purdue operation/login expectations.

### Task 6 — Student organizations

> Find a student organization you might join.

Success: finds BoilerLink and notices that availability or event scope may vary by campus.

### Task 7 — Crisis versus routine support

> In a hypothetical situation, a student needs immediate mental-health crisis guidance. Find the immediate action, then show where routine counseling information is listed.

Success: distinguishes 911/988 immediate actions from CAPS and routine support; understands BoilerCompass is a directory, not a crisis or counseling service.

### Task 8 — Campus filter

> Limit the directory to one campus or location, then return to all resources.

Success: finds Campus/location under More filters, recognizes the active filter, and clears it without losing orientation.

### Task 9 — Favorite storage

> Save a useful resource, then explain where you think it is stored and whether you needed an account.

Success: uses the heart/favorite action, finds Favorites, and understands that the item stays only in this browser/device with no account or cloud sync.

### Task 10 — Outdated link

> Imagine a listed destination is outdated. Show how you would report it, but do not submit anything.

Success: finds the correction action and recognizes that it opens GitHub in a new tab with resource context prefilled.

## Closing questions

1. What was easiest?
2. What was confusing or required rereading?
3. Was there a label you would rename?
4. Did any result look more official—or less official—than it actually was?
5. What task would you expect this site to support that you could not find?
6. Would you use BoilerCompass again? Why or why not?
